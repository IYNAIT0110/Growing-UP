**〔B.2｜案例：一个"好"字换来的醒悟〕**

Young Blake was a textbook womanizer. He had been through a failed marriage, and he admitted he had never really been faithful to any woman. After the divorce he met Lisa. **〔B.3｜醒悟的触发点，与二十多年后的结果〕**

So he apologized and asked for another chance. And so when she came back, it didn't take me long. I said, we're getting married. I was very decisive, and I had made my mind up. But it was her walking away. That's the point that gave me the clarity that this woman is my life's partner. To this day, their marriage has lasted more than twenty years.

> 后来 Lisa 交往了帅气多金的棒球球员。布莱克才幡然醒悟，觉得 Lisa 是自己的此生至爱，是他唯一愿意忠诚的女人，于是道歉求和。所以当她回来的时候，我没花多久就下了决心。我说：我们结婚吧。我非常果断，我已经下定了决心。但真正让我下定决心的，是她当初走了。就是那一刻让我看清：这个女人是我的人生伴侣。直到今天，他们的婚姻已经持续了 20 多年。

**〔B.4｜焦虑是怎么变成减分的〕**

The more people care about someone, the more easily they project the anxiety of "I cannot lose you". They second-guess the other person's mind, never dare to state what they actually need, and panic at the slightest coldness. You think it's caring. In their eyes, it may be the beginning of your appeal draining away.

> 很多人越在乎一个人，越容易表现出"我绝不能失去你"的焦虑。不停揣测对方心思，不敢表达真实需求，对方稍有冷落就惶恐不安。你以为这是在乎，但在对方眼里，这可能是吸引力迅速消散的开始。

**〔B.5｜最后通牒博弈：规则与推论〕**

There is a classic experiment in game theory called the ultimatum game. Say you're splitting a cake with a stranger: you cut, he chooses. After you cut two pieces, you ask him whether this one is okay. If he nods, you both eat. If he shakes his head, the waiter throws the whole cake in the bin, and neither of you gets anything. Now the knife is in your hand — you hold the power to allocate — and yet you don't dare cut too greedily. Cut yourself ninety percent, and he can decide you're screwing him over, flip the table and refuse. Then you both lose. So to keep any cake at all, you have to cut it more or less fairly — fifty-fifty, say. That's the ultimatum game.

> 博弈论中有一个非常经典的实验，叫做最后通牒博弈。假如你和陌生人分一块蛋糕：你负责切，他负责选。你切好两块之后，问他这块给你行不行。他点头，你们就按这个分法吃；但只要他摇头，服务员就会立刻把整块蛋糕扔进垃圾桶，你们俩谁也别想吃。这个时候，虽然刀在你手里，你有分配权，但你却不敢切得太贪心。假如你给自己切 90%，对方觉得你太欺负人，他就有权利掀桌子拒绝，那结果就是双输。所以为了保住自己的蛋糕，你不得不切得相对公平，比如说五五开。这就是最后通牒博弈。

**〔B.6｜博弈论说了什么关于公平的话〕**

It exposes something brutally simple: refusal and walking away are a right. And fairness is usually forced into existence by the ability to flip the table. When someone has no standing to refuse and cannot afford to leave, fair treatment rarely comes.

> 它揭示了一个非常残酷的现实：拒绝和离开是一种权利。而公平往往是靠掀桌子的能力逼出来的。当一个人没有拒绝的资格，也承担不起离开的代价时，他很难得到公平对待。

**〔B.7｜平衡的来源，与退让的真实代价〕**

You can choose to love him, understand him, keep investing — or you can choose to leave once your boundaries are trampled, and stop playing. Constant appeasement never buys you being valued. It steadily lowers the price of hurting you. Once the other side confirms your bottom line is that you have none, no amount of visible pain will earn you real respect.

> 关系中的平衡，不来自于两个人身份、财富和能力的完全相等，而来自于双方都拥有选择权。你可以选择爱他、理解他、继续投入，也可以选择在自己的边界被践踏后离开，不和他玩了。一味的示弱和退让不会换来珍惜。相反，它会不断降低对方伤害你的成本。当对方确认你的底线就是毫无底线时，你表达得再痛苦，也很难获得真正的尊重。

**〔B.8｜吸回、爱情轰炸，与有毒的怀旧〕**

They run another love bombing campaign, turn back into the tender, attentive version from the beginning, and let you believe they have finally seen the light — that the relationship really can restart. So you fall into a toxic nostalgia, endlessly replaying the best times between you. Those memories dilute and cover over the damage the relationship actually did. Strip it down: what you can't let go of isn't the person in front of you. It's the version of them you remember.

> 坚定不移地离开，不是欲擒故纵。它代表你非常清楚自己想要什么、能接受什么。我知道有很多姐妹在关系中挣扎，明明已经意识到关系有毒，但就是离不开。但这也许并不是你的错。因为具有强烈自恋和操控倾向的伴侣，往往非常懂得怎么把你重新拉回深渊。心理学中把这种行为称作"吸回"。它会一直在你身边徘徊、试探，观察你的反应。它还会再次对你展开爱情轰炸，变成恋爱初期温柔体贴的样子，让你误以为它终于醒悟，关系真的能够重新开始。于是你会陷入一种有毒的怀旧，不停回忆你们之间最美好的时光。这些回忆冲淡、覆盖了关系中真实存在的创伤和痛苦。究其本质，你舍不得的根本就不是眼前这个人，而是你记忆中那个美好的他。

**〔B.9｜鳄鱼法则：该断的脚，与不该掰的嘴〕**

Never try to fix this through communication. Understand that speaking up is the need of the person with less power, and silence is the privilege of the one with more. The moment you're still asking the person who hurt you for an explanation, still arguing the rights and wrongs, you have already fallen into a bottomless pit of self-drain. Economic psychology has a vivid metaphor for this: the crocodile rule. Picture walking along when a hungry crocodile lunges out and clamps down on your foot. Anyone thinking clearly reacts the same way: give up the foot if you must, tear yourself free, and run. And what does someone trapped in the emotional version do? They don't run. They bend down and try to pry the crocodile's jaws open.

> 千万不要试图通过沟通解决问题。你要明白，表达是下位者的需求，沉默是上位者的权利。当你还在试图跟那个伤害你的人要一个说法、试图掰扯清楚对错的时候，你其实已经掉进了一个无尽内耗的困局。经济心理学中有一个很形象的比喻，叫做鳄鱼法则。想象一下你正在走路，突然一只饥饿的鳄鱼窜了出来，死死咬住了你的脚。一个理智清醒的人，第一反应绝对是：哪怕舍弃这只脚，也要拼命挣脱，赶紧逃命。但陷入情绪陷阱的人会怎么做呢？他们不仅不跑，反而会弯下腰，伸手去掰鳄鱼的嘴。

**〔B.10｜断开之后的具体动作，与三条正面原则〕**

In a toxic relationship, the person who keeps stonewalling you, exploiting you, trampling your boundaries — that is the cold-blooded crocodile. The only method that works is to leave for good and cut contact, refusing to re-enter the old loop. No more meeting in person. Block them on every platform. Don't give them another opening to mess with your head. Don't waste another sliver of energy or another second on them. First, kindness. Always open with goodwill, and don't pre-load a partner as an enemy because you were hurt before. The moment they start acting out — PUA tactics, keeping you as a backup while they shop around — switch into counter-mode immediately: withdraw attention, freeze the relationship, stop investing. Kindness without fangs is called weakness. They have to know where your boundaries are, and that your goodwill carries a price. Finally, clarity. Don't make them guess. Build a simple behavioural reflex: treat me well and there's a reward; treat me badly and there's a whip. Swallowing your grievances does not buy real love. Clear boundaries and a clear self are what attract people on your frequency. Healthy love isn't one person subsidising the other. It's two independent souls conspiring on value.

> 在有毒的亲密关系里，那个不断冷暴力你、压榨你，甚至践踏你底线的人，就是这条冷血的鳄鱼。真正有效的方法，就是坚定地离开和断联，不再回到旧循环。现实中不要再见面，所有的社交媒体软件全部屏蔽拉黑，不要再给对方干扰你心智的机会。不要再为他浪费一丝一毫的心力、一分一秒的时间。第一，善良。开局永远先释放善意，不要因为过去受过伤，就把伴侣预设成敌人。一旦对方开始作，比如说 PUA、骑驴找马，你必须马上立刻开启反击模式：撤回关注、冷冻关系、停止投入。没有獠牙的善良叫做软弱。你必须让对方知道你的边界，知道你的好是有价格的。最后，清晰性。不要让对方猜，要建立简单的行为反射：对我好等于有糖吃，对我坏就有鞭子抽。委曲求全换不来真爱。清晰的边界和自我，才能吸引同频的人。健康的爱情，不是一方对另外一方的供养，而是两个独立灵魂的价值共谋。

**〔C.1 ｜ 从案例到博弈论：退出权是怎么变成公平的〕**

**〔C.2 ｜ 从博弈论到边界：公平的价格〕**

There is a classic experiment in game theory called the ultimatum game. Say you're splitting a cake with a stranger: you cut, he chooses. After you cut two pieces, you ask him whether this one is okay. If he nods, you both eat. If he shakes his head, the waiter throws the whole cake in the bin, and neither of you gets anything. Now the knife is in your hand — you hold the power to allocate — and yet you don't dare cut too greedily. Cut yourself ninety percent, and he can decide you're screwing him over, flip the table and refuse. Then you both lose. So to keep any cake at all, you have to cut it more or less fairly — fifty-fifty, say. That's the ultimatum game. It exposes something brutally simple: refusal and walking away are a right. And fairness is usually forced into existence by the ability to flip the table. When someone has no standing to refuse and cannot afford to leave, fair treatment rarely comes.

<details>
<summary>中文自查（读完英文再展开）</summary>

博弈论中有一个非常经典的实验，叫做最后通牒博弈。假如你和陌生人分一块蛋糕：你负责切，他负责选。你切好两块之后，问他这块给你行不行。他点头，你们就按这个分法吃；但只要他摇头，服务员就会立刻把整块蛋糕扔进垃圾桶，你们俩谁也别想吃。这个时候，虽然刀在你手里，你有分配权，但你却不敢切得太贪心。假如你给自己切 90%，对方觉得你太欺负人，他就有权利掀桌子拒绝，那结果就是双输。所以为了保住自己的蛋糕，你不得不切得相对公平，比如说五五开。这就是最后通牒博弈。它揭示了一个非常残酷的现实：拒绝和离开是一种权利。而公平往往是靠掀桌子的能力逼出来的。当一个人没有拒绝的资格，也承担不起离开的代价时，他很难得到公平对待。

</details>
