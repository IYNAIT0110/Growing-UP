### C.2 （警觉策略的来源与后果｜2:05–2:40）

So you can imagine: suppose that from childhood on, my parents were always arguing. What game strategy would I breed? Very distinctly, I'd grow one called vigilance. My ears would perk up very high. The house has its own winds and rustling grass; the moment I hear it — ah, it's over. I come back, I see a shadow, and before she's even started arguing, my mother has just one line — "How are the slippers placed?" — and I shut the bedroom door. I know it has nothing to do with the slippers. I know somebody must have gotten going, and another round of arguing is about to start. I'm on alert, I know it's over, I don't want to be drawn into it, so I quietly close the door. And so he becomes more sensitive; his perception of the mood and the emotions around him becomes sharper. He might even grow a somewhat people-pleasing personality — and through all that emotional turmoil, this becomes that steady-state strategy. Then when he's in a relationship, will he be cautious and timid? Will he always place himself lower? Will he always accommodate other people's ideas? Will he always be picking up on the mood — and even after picking it up, find it hard to express it easily? Because at home, the moment he expressed it, he got scolded — so in a relationship he won't express it easily. So how will he be — won't he also easily get stuck in internal churn? And then he thinks, what does he think of me? Is what I'm doing good or not, right or wrong? So we say it's not just imitation. It is essentially the shaping of the environment; through the constant adjustment of game strategies, it shapes your stable-state personality.

### C.3 （重建自由人格的路径｜2:40–3:47）

So — is there freedom? Yes, there is. The environment after the family of origin can actually be freely chosen. But this is very hard. What does it require? It requires awareness, it requires knowledge of psychology. It requires constant self-criticism, even self-subversion. You have to realize that the many ways and principles of handling things that you've been especially proud of since childhood — even revering as truth — require argument, not assertion. You need to know, in these processes, your gains and losses, your costs, your rewards, and the possibly sorrowful parts you paid that you haven't yet noticed. So these environments together shape a constant self-criticism and self-reflection. This is the cornerstone for us to rebuild a free personality. At the same time, as you were just saying, you also have to read — and then you can find a direction for your ideal personality. "I feel I'm a bit too caught up in internal churn; I feel I'm too much of a nice guy — no, that won't do." "I particularly long for a kind of personality that can let go when the moment calls for it, that cuts decisively, and is thoroughly free and easy on the outside." Sure — then I'll take that as my ideal personality. Then, in the environments I can choose from here on, I need the ones that can give me positive feedback toward that personality. You can get as close as you can to friends like that. In many of the settings where you spend time together, you can naturally come into contact with some of that. You don't need to think everything through so deeply and in such detail. You can let yourself spend more time in the environments where you're free to be yourself. And in these relationships, bit by bit, you bring about a change in your environment. So when the environment changes, you change — and your future changes too.
